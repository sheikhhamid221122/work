{# Zahid Brothers (users.id 74a2012a-8bf8-46e8-94fd-611e748e7c35) -- sales tax invoice. This started as a replica of their own sheet, `templates/user-invoices/zahid_brothers.pdf` (invoice 092606 of 10.09.2026) -- an Excel form: one ruled frame around a centred masthead, two columns of label-and-rule fields, a thirteen-column items table padded to ten lines, and a signature block. The client has since asked for the same invoice to look modern, so the ruled form is gone and the content is not. See "What the restyle kept" below before changing anything here. What the restyle kept --------------------- Every field and every column their form had, in the order it had them: * the masthead artwork, untouched -- it is their letterhead * Invoice Number, Invoice Date, FBR Invoice No., Reference, Term of Sales, and any custom field that carries a value * supplier: name, address (two lines), Phone, NTN No., Sales Tax Registration No. * buyer: name, address (two lines), CNIC No, NTN if any, Sales Tax Reg. No. * the thirteen items columns, with their headings spelled as theirs are: Sr.No. | HS CODE | HS CODE DESCRIPTION | Item Type | Product Code & Description | Qty | UOM | Part Unit | Unit Rate | Gross Amount | Sales Tax | Further Sales Tax if ANY | Value Including Sales Tax * the totals, the signature rules, Name & Designation and Stamp * the FBR marks: invoice number, Digital Invoicing logo, QR code What the restyle dropped, deliberately -------------------------------------- * **The measured geometry.** The old table was 174.6 mm of columns measured off the vertical rules in their PDF, in 9.45 mm rows. That was the client's earlier ask -- "exactly the size we set" -- and the modern ask replaces it. Columns are now proportioned for legibility across the 186 mm of an A4 with 12 mm margins. * **The ten-row form, down to five.** Their Excel sheet always printed ten lines and dashed the empty ones. The restyle dropped the padding altogether and the client asked for the shape back at five, which is what the `range(items | length + 1, 6)` loop below the items does: a one-line invoice prints its line and four ruled blanks. FIVE IS A FLOOR, NOT A CAP -- a longer invoice runs on and pads nothing. A blank row is a dash in all thirteen columns and never a 0.00, which would read as a real figure of nought. * **The frame.** One hairline rule under the masthead and the table's own rules do the work the box used to. Rules the client gave, all still here ------------------------------------- * **13 digits is a CNIC, anything else is an NTN.** Their form has a "CNIC No" row and an "NTN if any" row; the payload has one field, `buyerNTNCNIC`. See `buyer_is_cnic`. * **UOM prints its first comma-separated term** -- "Numbers" for FBR's "Numbers, pieces, units". The first *term*, not the first word, or "Square Metre" would print as "Square". * **Item Type is the sale type** the line was filed under. * **Part Unit is their own figure**, typed on the invoice form, and it is what the line is priced by: Part Unit x Unit Rate = Gross Amount, with the quantity a weight that takes no part in it. A line carrying none falls back to the derivation this column used to be, Gross / Unit Rate. A typed 0 is a figure and prints as one, which is why this tests for a missing value and not a falsy one. * **Product Code & Description prints the description alone.** The code in that column is part of the text they type; the separate box printed this application's own P-0001 series in front of it and was removed. * **The total is spelled out.** Their old sheet never carried an amount in words; the client asked for one with the restyle. It is app.py's `amountInWords`, derived from the same total printed beside it, so the two can never disagree, and the band is left off when there is nothing to spell. * **Figures print to two decimals.** Their sheet rounded the line columns to whole rupees and only the total to two, so their lines visibly did not add up to their total. What is filed has two decimals, so this prints two and the column adds up. * **Phone, NTN No. and Sales Tax Registration No. are fixed text**, in FIXED_* below, at the client's request. Note the cost: if their registration changes this sheet prints the old number while the new one is filed. Three lines to change, and the first thing to check if FBR ever disputes a seller identifier. Name and address stay live. Added, and must never be removed -------------------------------- 1. the FBR invoice number -- in the document details, once only; the QR carries it too 2. the FBR Digital Invoicing logo 3. the QR code 4. the buyer's sales tax registration number -- their form had rows for the buyer's CNIC and NTN but none for the registration, and it belongs on the face of a sales tax invoice Contract: rendered by app.py with data, qr_base64, client_logo_url, fbr_logo_url, username, settings, theme.
