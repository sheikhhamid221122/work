Deliberate departures from the original --------------------------------------- * Their items table carries only S.No., Description, Qty, Unit, Rate and Value. HS code, the sales tax on each line and the value including tax are required on the face of a sales tax invoice, so four columns are added: HS Code, S.Tax Rate, Sales Tax and Value Incl. Sales Tax. The client has not asked for any of them to come off; the one waiver on record is F.K. Printers' HS code, and only because they asked for it twice. * The value rules in the identifier column are longer than theirs (about 42 mm against 24 mm). The FBR invoice number is 27 characters and has to stay on one line, and rules of two different lengths in one column read as a mistake. * Figures are comma-grouped to two decimals everywhere, including the quantity, which their sheet prints whole. Every other replica does the same, and a quantity is not always a whole count of pieces. * "Special Excise Duty" prints the federal excise actually payable on the lines rather than a hard-coded nought, so a line that carries FED cannot show zero. The sales tax rate beside "Sales Tax" is taken from the goods and shown only when every line shares one rate; theirs was typed "(18%)". * Time of Supply is theirs to state, so it prints a custom field of that name when the client enters one. With no such field it falls back to the clock time the PDF is produced (fixed +05:00) rather than leaving their row blank -- that is the print time, not the time of supply, so tell them to type the real one in when it matters. * The buyer's telephone number is on their sheet but is not in the invoice payload, so it is not printed. The seller's own phone and e-mail are not in the payload either and are fixed text below -- change them here if theirs change. * Their sheet rules the buyer's address over two lines. Here it is one ruled cell deep enough for two, so a longer address wraps instead of running off the end of a fixed rule.
